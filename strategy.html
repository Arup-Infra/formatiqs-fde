Why The FDE Factory?

1.1 The market gap

Technology without business context: technical teams may understand platforms but lack a deep view of the processes they are expected to transform.

Business problems without engineering ownership: leaders identify opportunities but struggle to convert them into requirements, architecture and deployable solutions.

AI experiments without production deployment: proof-of-concepts encounter integration, data, governance, security, reliability and adoption barriers.

Talent without deployment readiness: conventional training may not prepare professionals to discover problems, engage stakeholders and deploy solutions.

Fragmented service delivery: clients coordinate multiple providers across discovery, process consulting, engineering, integration, testing and adoption.

The FormatiQs Business Architecture

Engine 1 — FDE Awareness & Market Development

Purpose: Create awareness, understanding and demand for the FDE profession and operating model.

Scope: Talent audiences: students, graduates, IT professionals, business analysts and consultants. Business audiences: founders, CEOs, CXOs, functional leaders, universities, technology companies and enterprises.

Activities: awareness campaigns, leadership workshops, industry roundtables, university engagement, opportunity discovery workshops and organisational readiness assessments. Outcome: a qualified talent pipeline and an informed business opportunity pipeline.

Engine 2 — FDE Academy, Training & Certification

Purpose: Build a structured talent development pathway from student to enterprise-ready FDE professional.

Scope: Learning pathways, practice labs, case simulations, competency assessments, certification and mentoring.

The academy uses competency-led progression and practice-based learning. It aggregates relevant learning resources and partner content rather than recreating every course.

Engine 3 — FDE Talent Sourcing & Qualification

Purpose: Build a reliable pipeline of qualified FDE professionals for global deployment.

Scope: Academic, professional and experienced-talent sourcing.

Candidates pass technical, business, analytical, architecture, practical simulation and communication assessments. Qualified profiles enter the FormatiQs FDE Talent Registry.

Engine 4 — FDE Deployment & Engineering Services

Purpose: Deploy qualified FDE professionals and multidisciplinary teams to solve client problems.

Scope: Individual FDE deployment, dedicated FDE pods, managed FDE teams, project-based deployment and global hybrid deployment.

FormatiQs manages scoping, architecture, talent matching, onboarding, engineering, testing, deployment, adoption and value tracking.

Engine 5 — AI, Agentic & Product Engineering Factory

Purpose: Provide end-to-end engineering services to global clients.

Scope: Discovery, process architecture, AI and software engineering, data and integration, product engineering, QA, DevOps and change enablement.

The factory builds production-oriented solutions with clear architecture, quality and deployment controls.

Engine 6 — Managed Services & Continuous Engineering

Purpose: Establish recurring revenue and long-term global client relationships.

Scope: Managed FDE teams, AI application support, agent monitoring, platform enhancements, integration support, QA and optimisation.

The objective is to move beyond one-time implementation projects towards long-term engineering and transformation partnerships.

Strategic revenue priorities

Near term: paid discovery, engineering projects, talent sourcing and institutional programmes.

Medium term: recurring FDE pods, academy cohorts and managed services.

Long term: global accounts, platform subscriptions, multi-year managed engineering partnerships and international deployment.

The commercial model should prioritise recurring engagements rather than depend exclusively on one-time training or recruitment revenue.

Global Market Development Strategy

Phase 1 — India Foundation

Establish methodology and brand; build core engineering capability; develop academic and industry partnerships; run pilot FDE cohorts; secure initial paid client projects.

Phase 2 — Global Client Acquisition

Identify AI and technology companies seeking implementation capacity; build relationships with global consulting and technology firms; offer discovery, engineering and embedded FDE pods; establish reference projects.

Phase 3 — Global Deployment Network

Build international partner relationships and local client channels; establish compliant cross-border contracting and deployment models; expand remote, hybrid and onsite delivery.

Phase 4 — Global FDE Factory

Operate a scalable talent and engineering pipeline; build multi-country client accounts; expand managed services; develop platform-led delivery and partner ecosystems.

International expansion must account for local employment, taxation, immigration, data protection, IP and contractual requirements.
